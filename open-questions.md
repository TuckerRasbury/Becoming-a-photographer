## Pricing

- [ ] **Q1 — Adobe subscription tier**
  Which Adobe plan? Photography Plan (~$20/mo) or Creative Cloud All Apps (~$60/mo)? Decide tier, then decide whether to itemize in proposals or bake into rates.

- [ ] **Q2 — Cost of doing business (CODB) baseline**
  Full monthly overhead calculation: Adobe + Pixiset + portfolio site + phone (pro-rated) + backup storage + business cards + gear amortization + insurance (if applicable). This is the floor beneath your $200/hr floor.

- [ ] **Q3 — Tier 2 licensing rate card**
  Build 3–5 use cases with defensible price ranges. Reference tools: Getty Images pricing matrix, fotoQuote, ASMP rate guides, PhotoShelter licensing PDF.

- [ ] **Q4 — Documentary / protest image licensing**
  If a news outlet, publisher, or nonprofit wants to license an image shot at a protest or community event, what do you charge? Editorial use has different rules than commercial use.

- [ ] **Q5 — Copyright registration**
  US Copyright Office batch registration (~$65) unlocks statutory damages if someone infringes. Worth doing once you have a body of work. When is the right time?

## Contracts

- [ ] **Q6 — Cancellation fee structure**
  Deposit held at any cancellation. What % of remaining balance is owed if client cancels within 72 hours? Within 24 hours? Same-day?

- [ ] **Q7 — Rescheduling limit**
  How many reschedules before a fee kicks in? What is the rescheduling fee?

- [ ] **Q8 — Model / subject release form**
  Build a release form for portrait and event clients. Decide policy for documentary / street / protest work where subjects are in public.

- [ ] **Q9 — Liability insurance**
  General liability for photographers: ~$200–500/year in California. Some venues require a certificate of insurance. Is this needed now or later?

- [ ] **Q10 — Equipment insurance**
  Current kit worth ~$500–800. Dream kit could be $3,000+. Check renter's/homeowner's policy, or look at Full Frame Insurance for dedicated coverage.

- [ ] **Q11 — Permit responsibility clause**
  Confirm: client is responsible for location permits and access. Isaac is responsible for public-space awareness. This needs to be explicit in contracts.

- [ ] **Q12 — Payment terms**
  When is final payment due — before delivery, at delivery, or net 14? Define clearly.

- [ ] **Q13 — Late payment clause**
  What happens if a client doesn't pay on time? Interest? Withheld delivery?

## Business Systems

- [ ] **Q14 — Pre-shoot questionnaire / creative brief**
  Fields: event type, date/time/location, shot list priorities, key people, restrictions, intended image use, preferred delivery format. Build in Pixiset or Google Forms.

- [ ] **Q15 — Invoicing and contract platform**
  HoneyBook and Dubsado combine contracts, invoicing, and client comms. Worth evaluating before building from scratch in Pixiset alone.

- [ ] **Q16 — Community rate criteria**
  Define: registered nonprofit? mutual aid context? personal connection? private vs. published use? Having criteria makes the discount principled and consistent.

- [ ] **Q17 — Portfolio site decision**
  Format vs. Cargo. Cargo may be stronger signal for documentary/editorial identity. Decision pending.

- [ ] **Q18 — Pixiset + portfolio site workflow**
  Portfolio site = attraction (show work, get inquiry). Pixiset = delivery (galleries, sales, downloads). Define how clients move between them.

## Audience & Strategy

- [ ] **Q19 — Public identity statement**
  Isaac has a clear internal identity (constitution Section 0) but no 3-sentence version a stranger can find in 10 seconds. Draft the public version: who you are, what you photograph, and why someone should care. Apply it consistently to Instagram bio, portfolio site homepage, and the opening of the standard client proposal. This is the highest-leverage missing piece in the repo.

- [ ] **Q20 — Editorial target list**
  Identify 3–5 local and regional outlets in Southern California that publish civic/documentary photography (protest, labor, community organizing). Research each outlet's pitch or submission process. Set a target date for a first submission. See `strategy/audience-and-sustainability.md` Section 5.

- [ ] **Q21 — Shoot-type emotional cost taxonomy**
  Build a simple High / Medium / Lower taxonomy of shoot types by emotional and physical intensity — e.g., High (protest, labor action, grief/illness contexts), Medium (weddings, celebrations, full-day events), Lower (headshots, portraits, commercial). Use this taxonomy to inform scheduling decisions alongside pricing. Answer this before accepting retainer clients or booking more than 2 weeks out.

- [ ] **Q22 — Calendar protection minimums**
  Decide: what is the maximum number of shoot days per month that is sustainable? How many days per month are protected from booking by default? Define these as hard rules, not preferences. Answer this alongside Q21 — the two questions are linked.
