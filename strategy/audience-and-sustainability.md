# ITR Photography — Audience Strategy & Sustainability

*Version 1.0 · June 2026*

This document operates one layer above the constitution. The constitution governs how you serve clients. This document governs how you build the practice that attracts them, and how you protect yourself while doing it.

The open questions raised here are tracked in `open-questions.md` (Q19–Q23).

---

## 1. Audience Before Infrastructure — DECIDED

**Principle:** The pricing system, contracts, and workflows are the back-of-house. A restaurant needs a kitchen, but the kitchen doesn't feed anyone until people know the restaurant exists. The constitution is complete enough to take clients today — the priority now shifts to making sure the right people know you're available.

**What this means in practice:**
- Do not spend more time refining the constitution or workflows until Q19–Q23 are addressed
- Time spent on public presence now compounds; time spent on contract polish does not
- The ordering is: public identity statement → discovery channel → editorial path → then continue refining infrastructure

## 2. Theory of Discovery — DECIDED

**Principle:** Instagram and a portfolio site serve fundamentally different audiences and must be maintained with different goals in mind. Conflating them — or treating one as a substitute for the other — leaves one audience without a front door.

| Platform | Audience | Purpose | Optimized for |
|----------|----------|---------|---------------|
| Instagram | Strangers who don't know you exist yet | Discovery | Shareability, consistent voice, emotional resonance |
| Portfolio site | People who Googled you and are evaluating you | Legitimacy | Clarity, identity legibility, contact path |
| Pixiset | Clients who already hired you | Delivery | Gallery access, download permissions |

**What this means in practice:**
- Instagram content should be selected and captioned with a stranger in mind — someone who has never heard of you and is deciding in 10 seconds whether to follow
- Portfolio site content should be selected with a potential client or editor in mind — someone qualifying you before reaching out
- These require different curation logic; do not cross-post indiscriminately

## 3. Two-Audience Model — DECIDED

**Principle:** Two distinct audiences need to be built simultaneously, for different reasons, through different channels.

**Professional audience** — editors, organizations, nonprofits, businesses:
- Finds you via Google, portfolio site, publication credits, referrals from known contacts
- Evaluates: identity legibility, consistency, past client/publication list
- Converts via: proposal process (existing workflows cover this)
- Key signal: a publication byline, an org credit, a named past client

**Community audience** — followers, peers, mutual connections, future word-of-mouth clients:
- Finds you via Instagram, shared posts, events they attended, community circles
- Evaluates: trust, shared values, quality of work seen in context
- Converts via: DM inquiry, referral, or community rate conversation
- Key signal: presence, consistency, and a voice that resonates

**What this means in practice:**
- Maintain separate mental models for what content serves each audience
- Community audience content is not lesser — it is the discovery engine for the professional audience
- See Q19 (open-questions.md) — both audiences need the public identity statement

## 5. Press / Editorial Path — DECIDED as orientation; OPEN (Q20) for specific targets

**Principle:** For documentary photographers, reputation travels through publication, not through commercial client volume. One byline in a local outlet that covers civic life does more credibility work than twenty headshot sessions. Editorial credits compound in a way that headshots do not.

The path in rough order:
1. Community organizations formally crediting your work in their communications
2. Local digital outlets covering protest, labor, and civic life in SoCal
3. Regional publications with civic/documentary sections
4. Pitch-based editorial placements

"As seen in [publication]" signals to a Track B organization that you are credible in exactly the work they want to hire you for. That signal is not available from a headshot portfolio.

**What this means in practice:**
- Frame editorial submissions as reputation infrastructure — not a separate income stream (editorial rates are low; the ROI is the credit and the compounding visibility)
- A relevant clip strengthens any Track B proposal to a civic org or nonprofit
- See Q20 in `open-questions.md` for the first concrete task: identifying 3–5 target outlets
- Agents: when building a proposal for a civic organization, ask whether Isaac has a relevant published clip — if yes, reference it

## 6. Sustainability — OPEN (Q21, Q22)

**Principle:** Protest photography, labor actions, and emotionally charged family events carry real physical and psychological cost that does not show up in the hourly rate. The risk is not financial — it is burning out in two years instead of shooting at sixty. Deliberate structure around which work you take, at what pace, and with what recovery time is a strategic constraint, not a scheduling preference.

Two distinct problems to solve:

**Problem A — Shoot-type cost taxonomy (Q21)**
Not all shoot hours are equivalent. A 4-hour protest with police lines costs differently than a 4-hour headshot session — not in what you charge, but in what it costs you. Until there is a taxonomy, scheduling decisions treat all work as equivalent and the calendar fills in ways that deplete rather than sustain.

**Problem B — Calendar protection minimums (Q22)**
Without a hard rule, high-demand periods will fill every available day. Good intentions do not protect time. The rule needs to define: maximum shoot days per month, and minimum days protected from booking by default.

**What this means in practice:**
- See Q21 and Q22 in `open-questions.md`
- These questions should be answered before accepting retainer clients or booking more than 2 weeks out
- Agents: do not suggest or schedule back-to-back high-intensity shoots (protest, labor action, emotionally charged family contexts) without flagging Q21 and Q22 if those questions are still open

## 7. The Guiding Question — DECIDED (Q23 — June 4, 2026)

**Answer: Secondary income — fun, complementary to the primary career, intentionally bounded.**

Photography does not carry the pressure of being the primary livelihood. It stays enjoyable precisely because it doesn't have to. This is option (a): secondary income alongside data analytics and civic tech, not trending toward replacing either.

**What this means now — downstream governance:**

| Question | Governed by Q23 answer |
|----------|------------------------|
| Q1 — Adobe tier | Photography Plan (~$20/mo) is sufficient. All Apps is overkill at this scale. |
| Q15 — Invoicing platform | HoneyBook/Dubsado are CRM-heavy for secondary income volume. Hold off. Simple invoicing until volume makes a CRM genuinely necessary. |

| Q17 — Portfolio site | A clean, well-done Format or Cargo site at a basic tier is right. Do not over-invest in platform features. |

**What does not change:**
- Copyright, RAW file policy, and licensing principles apply regardless of income tier
- Q19 (identity statement) — still the highest-leverage next step; applies at any scale
- Q21 (shoot-type taxonomy) — sustainability still matters; secondary income does not mean unlimited availability

**For agents:** Q23 is resolved. When advising on platform investment, gear spending, or marketing scale, use this answer as the governing constraint — keep it simple, keep it sustainable, keep it fun.
